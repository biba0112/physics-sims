How to use this simulation

  1. Set the launch speed, angle (negative = thrown downward) and height. Press ▶ Launch, or drag the time probe to any moment of the flight.
  2. Set the values with the sliders, or type a value in the box next to a slider and press Enter. A value outside the allowed range is set to the nearest allowed value.
  3. Vectors draws the velocity every 0.2 s along the path; Components adds vx (purple) and vy (blue).
  4. The velocity diagram (middle column) draws all those velocities from one point. Their tips lie on a vertical line, equally spaced: every 0.2 s the velocity changes by the same amount, Δv = gΔt, straight down.
  5. ● Record reading saves the probe values. The Data tab plots vy against t: gradient = −g, intercept = uy.
  6. ◂ 0.1 s / 0.1 s ▸ (or the ← → keys) step the motion back and forward and pause it, so you can discuss each moment: the velocity and acceleration arrows stay on the screen. Graph tools: Area shades the area under a velocity graph (= displacement) or an acceleration graph (= change in velocity) up to that moment and writes what the area is (its name and value) inside it; Tangent draws the tangent there (gradient of a position graph = velocity, of a velocity graph = acceleration).
  7. Work through the Tasks tab. Write explanations on your worksheet.

The physics

  • vx = u cos θ (constant) · vy = u sin θ − gt
  • speed v = √(vx² + vy²) · direction φ = tan⁻¹(vy / vx)
  • At height y: vy² = uy² − 2g(y − h), so the speed depends only on the height — the same on the way up and on the way down.
  • Acceleration: a = Δv/Δt = g downward, everywhere on the path.

Model: no air resistance (see the Air resistance simulation); g = 9.81 (Earth), 1.62 (Moon), 3.71 (Mars) m s⁻².
