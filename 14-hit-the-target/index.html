How to use this simulation

  1. Choose a target: a cup on the floor, a basketball hoop or a window in a wall. Set its distance D and height, or press 🎯 New target for a random one.
  2. Set the values with the sliders, or type a value in the box next to a slider and press Enter. A value outside the allowed range is set to the nearest allowed value.
  3. Set the launch speed u, angle θ (0° = horizontal, negative = downward) and launch height h.
  4. Use the shot planner (middle column): it does the working for your settings — time to reach the target distance, then the height of the ball there. Change u or θ until the ball arrives at the target height.
  5. Press ▶ Shoot. A cup or hoop counts only if the ball comes down into it; a window counts if the ball passes through it.
  6. ◂ 0.1 s / 0.1 s ▸ (or the ← → keys) step the motion back and forward and pause it, so you can discuss each moment: the velocity and acceleration arrows stay on the screen. Graph tools: Area shades the area under a velocity graph (= displacement) or an acceleration graph (= change in velocity) up to that moment and writes what the area is (its name and value) inside it; Tangent draws the tangent there (gradient of a position graph = velocity, of a velocity graph = acceleration).
  7. The Attempts tab plots your attempts on a u–θ graph with the needed-speed curve: every point on the curve hits.

The physics

  • Horizontal: t = D / (u cos θ) — the time to reach the target distance.
  • Vertical: y = h + u sin θ · t − ½gt² — the height of the ball at that time.
  • Together: y = h + D tan θ − gD² / (2u² cos² θ). Set y equal to the target height and solve for u (one answer) or for θ (usually two answers: a low and a high shot).

Model: no air resistance; g = 9.81 m s⁻². Cup width 0.30 m, hoop width 0.46 m, window height 0.80 m.
